On parting

I I was twelve, so was he. I was a kid, so was he. He drowned in water; I didn’t Did he know what death is? I didn’t Then they put him in a coffin, The smallest I have seen. Someone hastened to his room, Threw all that belonged to him. Homework we finished together, Drawings I made for class, Torn up, ended in the backyard. I remained silent. Neighbours whispered and gasped, I remained speechless. Tears and silent screams, Half-eaten bread I left on the plate, Relentless memories. II The very many calls in the morning, All that said, My grandmother has gone. But she hardly left home, thought I. She then waited for me for the last time, In the veranda – like always. They wrapped her in white, Kept her on the floor. Yet I know she hated both. I hurried to my room, Stumbled on the staircase. Glanced through the window, There was a crowd, There was fire. And then there was nothing. My grandmother had gone.
